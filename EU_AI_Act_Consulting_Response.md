# EU AI Act — Consulting Response

## Client Use Cases

This document contains four client briefs prepared for partner review.

The intended AI Act category for each case is intentionally hidden. The purpose is to allow the reviewing consultant to infer the appropriate risk classification from the business context, AI behaviour, data/signals, people affected, and human involvement.

---

# Case 1

## Client Brief

A large retail company wants to improve employee retention across its stores. It plans to install AI-enabled cameras in staff areas and use facial and behavioural signals to estimate each employee's emotional state during the working day. The system would analyse expressions, posture, voice characteristics and other observable signals to determine whether an employee appears stressed, frustrated, disengaged or satisfied. Store managers would receive individual employee profiles and could use the scores when deciding who should receive additional supervision, performance discussions or other workplace interventions. Managers could override the system's recommendations, but the AI scores would remain part of the employee assessment record.

### ① Company

Large retail company operating multiple stores.

### ② Business Problem

The company wants to improve employee retention and identify workplace issues affecting employees.

### ③ AI Behaviour

The AI estimates individual employees' emotional states and produces profiles/scores that managers can use during workplace management.

### ④ Data / Signals

The system analyses facial expressions, posture, voice characteristics and other observable behavioural signals.

### ⑤ People Affected

Employees working in the company's stores.

### ⑥ Human Involvement

Store managers review the AI-generated scores and can override the system's recommendations, but the scores remain part of the employee assessment record.

Intended category: Prohibited
Why: The proposed workplace AI system attempts to infer employees' emotions from biometric/observable signals in the workplace, which falls within the EU AI Act's prohibited emotion-recognition practice, subject to the Act's specific exceptions.

# Case 2

## Client Brief

A national recruitment company wants to reduce the time recruiters spend screening applications for large-volume vacancies. It plans to introduce an AI system that analyses CVs, application answers and relevant professional information, then ranks candidates against the requirements of each vacancy. Recruiters would see a recommended shortlist and an explanation of the factors that influenced the ranking. A recruiter would review the shortlist before candidates are invited to interviews, and recruiters could reject or add candidates themselves. The company wants to use the system across several recruitment offices and retain the assessment results for future hiring campaigns.

### ① Company

National recruitment company operating across several recruitment offices.

### ② Business Problem

The company wants to reduce the time recruiters spend screening large numbers of job applications.

### ③ AI Behaviour

The AI analyses applications, evaluates candidates against vacancy requirements and ranks them to produce a recommended shortlist for recruiters.

### ④ Data / Signals

The system analyses CVs, application answers and relevant professional information provided as part of the recruitment process.

### ⑤ People Affected

Job applicants who are being considered for employment opportunities.

### ⑥ Human Involvement

Recruiters review the AI-generated shortlist before candidates are invited to interviews. Recruiters can reject candidates, add candidates or override the AI's recommendations.

Intended category: High-risk
Why: The system is intended to be used for recruitment and selection of natural persons, an employment-related use covered by the high-risk category in Annex III of the EU AI Act. The fact that a human recruiter reviews the output does not remove the system from the high-risk category.

# Case 3

## Client Brief

A telecommunications company wants to add an AI assistant to its customer service website to handle routine questions about contracts, billing, network coverage and technical support. Customers would type questions into a chat window and receive responses generated by the AI assistant. The assistant could retrieve information from the company's approved knowledge base and escalate more complex issues to a human service agent. The company wants the assistant to communicate in a natural, conversational style so that customers can complete simple support requests without waiting for an employee.

### ① Company

Telecommunications company providing customer services through its website.

### ② Business Problem

The company wants to handle routine customer-service requests more efficiently and reduce waiting times for customers.

### ③ AI Behaviour

The AI assistant answers customers' questions in a conversational format, using the company's approved knowledge base. It can also escalate more complex requests to a human service agent.

### ④ Data / Signals

The system processes the questions and information entered by customers and retrieves relevant information from the company's approved knowledge base.

### ⑤ People Affected

Customers using the company's website and interacting with the AI assistant.

### ⑥ Human Involvement

A human service agent can take over when the AI cannot adequately handle a more complex request.

Intended category: Limited risk / transparency
Why: The customer is interacting directly with an AI system, so the relevant AI Act issue is transparency: the company should make the interaction with the AI sufficiently clear to the user. The system is not automatically high-risk merely because it provides customer-service assistance.

# Case 4

## Client Brief

A mid-sized architecture company wants to help its design teams work more efficiently when preparing internal project materials. It plans to use an AI writing assistant to turn meeting notes and project summaries into first drafts of internal project updates, brainstorming notes and presentation outlines. Employees would review and edit the generated material before using it in their work. The system would not make decisions about employees, customers or suppliers, and the company would not use the generated text as an automated basis for contracts or other legal commitments. The company wants to make the tool available to its design and project-management teams as a productivity aid.

### ① Company

Mid-sized architecture company with design and project-management teams.

### ② Business Problem

The company wants to improve the efficiency of its teams when preparing internal project materials and reduce the time spent creating first drafts.

### ③ AI Behaviour

The AI writing assistant transforms meeting notes and project summaries into first drafts of project updates, brainstorming notes and presentation outlines.

### ④ Data / Signals

The system processes meeting notes, project summaries and other information provided by employees for the purpose of generating internal content.

### ⑤ People Affected

Primarily the company's employees who use the AI tool in their design and project-management work. The system does not make decisions about employees, customers or suppliers.

### ⑥ Human Involvement

Employees review and edit the AI-generated material before using it in their work. The AI output is therefore used as a productivity aid rather than as an autonomous decision-making system.
Intended category: Minimal risk
Why: The proposed use is a general productivity and content-generation application that does not fall into the prohibited or high-risk categories described in the scenario. It can generally be deployed without specific AI Act obligations arising from a risk classification, although other requirements such as GDPR, confidentiality, intellectual-property rules or consumer/professional obligations may still be relevant depending on the implementation.
